# Great Readings

A curated collection of field-defining primary texts, transformative monographs, and landmark essays in Rhetoric. These works illustrate how foundational thinkers have conceptualized persuasion, symbolic action, public discourse, and the construction of knowledge.

---

## Classical Primary Foundations

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [*Rhetoric*](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/rhetoric.html) | Aristotle | Landmark Treatise | The foundational treatise of Western rhetorical theory, defining rhetoric as the faculty of observing in any given case the available means of persuasion and codifying ethos, pathos, logos, and the three rhetorical genres. |
| [*Gorgias and Phaedrus*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1672) | Plato | Philosophical Dialogues | Plato's pivotal critique of manipulative sophistry in *Gorgias*, balanced by *Phaedrus*, which envisions a true philosophical rhetoric rooted in dialectic, truth, and psychological understanding of the soul. |
| *De Oratore* (On the Ideal Orator) | Cicero | Philosophical Dialogue | Cicero's crowning work on civic rhetoric, arguing that the ideal orator must unify broad philosophical erudition, law, ethical integrity, and stylistic eloquence in service of the republic. |
| *Institutio Oratoria* (Institutes of Oratory) | Quintilian | Pedagogical Treatise | The definitive twelve-volume Roman treatise on rhetorical education, establishing the lifelong ideal of the orator as "the good person speaking well" (*vir bonus dicendi peritus*). |

## Modern Theoretical & Philosophical Masterworks

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [*A Grammar of Motives*](https://archive.org/details/grammarofmotives0000burk) | Kenneth Burke | Monograph | Introduces Burke's dramatistic pentad (act, scene, agent, agency, purpose) and redefines rhetoric around symbolic action, human motives, and identification rather than mere persuasion. |
| *The New Rhetoric: A Treatise on Argumentation* | Chaïm Perelman and Lucie Olbrechts-Tyteca | Monograph | A monumental post-WWII renewal of dialectical and rhetorical theory, formulating the concept of the "universal audience" to establish a foundation for practical reasoning beyond formal logic. |
| [*The Uses of Argument*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/uses-of-argument/26CF801BC12004587B66778297D5567C) | Stephen E. Toulmin | Monograph | Replaces traditional syllogistic logic with a field-dependent layout of argument (claims, data, warrants, backings, qualifiers, rebuttals) that transformed argumentation studies. |
| *The Rhetoric of Fiction* | Wayne C. Booth | Monograph | A landmark study in narrative theory analyzing how authors employ rhetorical devices, value systems, and the "implied author" to control audience perception and ethical judgment. |
| *The Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere* | Jürgen Habermas | Monograph | A foundational historical and sociological investigation into the emergence, institutional conditions, and subsequent decline of rational-critical public discourse in democratic societies. |

## Seminal Theoretical & Critical Essays

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [*The Rhetorical Situation*](https://files.commons.gc.cuny.edu/wp-content/blogs.dir/8310/files/2021/08/Bitzer1968-the-rhetorical-situation.pdf) | Lloyd F. Bitzer | Landmark Paper | Formulates the classic modern definition of the rhetorical situation as a complex of persons, events, objects, and relations presenting an exigence, an audience, and constraints. |
| "The Myth of the Rhetorical Situation" | Richard E. Vatz | Landmark Paper | The essential philosophical counterpoint to Bitzer, arguing that situations do not dictate discourse; rather, rhetors actively construct meaning and assign salience through language. |
| "Genre as Social Action" | Carolyn R. Miller | Landmark Paper | Revolutionized Rhetorical Genre Studies by redefining genres not as static formal rules or textual taxonomies, but as typified rhetorical actions responding to recurrent situations. |
| "The 'Ideograph': A Link Between Rhetoric and Ideology" | Michael Calvin McGee | Landmark Paper | Demonstrates how high-order abstraction terms (e.g., "liberty," "equality," "property") function as structural building blocks of public consciousness and ideological commitment. |
| "Critical Rhetoric: Theory and Praxis" | Raymie E. McKerrow | Landmark Paper | Establishes a post-structuralist critical praxis oriented toward unmasking discourses of power, domination, and freedom in institutional and vernacular rhetoric. |

## Feminist, Cultural, and Decolonial Interventions

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Unspoken: A Rhetoric of Silence* | Cheryl Glenn | Monograph | Reconceptualizes silence from an absence of speech or symptom of disempowerment into a deliberate, strategic, and potent rhetorical instrument used across cultures. |
| *Rhetoric Retold: Regendering the Tradition from Antiquity Through the Renaissance* | Cheryl Glenn | Monograph | A ground-breaking historiographical recovery that uncovers and reinterprets women rhetors (such as Aspasia, Diotima, and Julian of Norwich) excluded from the canonical history of rhetoric. |
| "Stories Take Place: A Rhetoric of Land, Space, and Memory" | Malea Powell | Landmark Essay | A foundational decolonial intervention illustrating how Indigenous rhetorics weave space, land, and material practices into survival narratives that disrupt colonial archives. |
| *Publics and Counterpublics* | Michael Warner | Monograph / Collection | Investigates how modern publics are discursively brought into existence by circulation, and how marginalized counterpublics construct alternative arenas of identity and politics. |

## Rhetoric of Science, Technology, and Digital Media

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *The Rhetoric of Science* | Alan G. Gross | Monograph | A seminal contribution to the rhetoric of science, arguing that scientific claims, peer review, and discovery narratives rely intrinsically on rhetorical persuasion rather than unmediated reality. |
| *Health and the Rhetoric of Medicine: Diagnostic Realities* | Judy Z. Segal | Monograph | Examines how medical clinical encounters, diagnostic disease categories, and public health policies are rhetorically constructed, contested, and maintained. |
| [*Digital Rhetoric: Theory, Method, Practice*](https://www.digitalculture.org/books/digital-rhetoric/) | Douglas Eyman | Monograph | Synthesizes traditional rhetorical canons with digital humanities scholarship to establish a formal methodology for analyzing networked communication, interfaces, and online media. |
| *Persuasive Games: The Expressive Power of Videogames* | Ian Bogost | Monograph | Introduces the concept of "procedural rhetoric," theorizing how rule-based computational systems, game mechanics, and simulations persuade through interactive procedural logic. |
